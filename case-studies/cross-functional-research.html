Collaboration · Case study

One problem, one room

A discovery program that aligned product, design, and engineering around user evidence — before commitment locked in.

Context

A high-stakes initiative had fractured: product framed it as growth, engineering as tech debt, design as a UX overhaul. Research existed, but it lived in siloed decks and Slack threads. The risk was building three different solutions to three different problems — all labeled with the same project name.

Evangelising research is not slides — it is getting the right people to hear the same evidence at the same time.

My role

I designed the discovery cadence, facilitated joint sessions with users present (remote and assisted), and translated observations into decision-ready artifacts. I coached ICs on asking non-leading questions and on capturing notes that engineering could trust.

Approach

Shared questions. Before the first session, product, design, and engineering co-wrote the questions we needed answered — and what would change if we learned X versus Y. That turned research from entertainment into a contract.

Observe together. We ran paired sessions so PM and tech leads heard hesitation, workarounds, and delight firsthand — reducing “users want” debates that usually rely on secondhand summaries.

Synthesis as a team sport. Affinity mapping and opportunity sketching happened in a single working session with clear facilitation rules. Disagreement moved from opinion to labeled evidence on the wall (virtual or physical).

Aligned framing one problem statement
Joint observation product, design, engineering
Faster convergence fewer late pivots

Outcome

The group converged on a smaller, sharper scope that addressed the primary user failure mode — validated by follow-up testing — and deferred vanity improvements that had been consuming calendar without evidence. Engineering estimates stabilized because constraints were no longer reinterpreted every sprint.

The format became a template for other initiatives: short, intensive discovery windows with explicit decision outputs rather than open-ended “learn more” phases.

What I would tune next time

Pre-brief exec sponsors with a one-page “how we will decide” summary so leadership expectations match the evidence window — and formalize a single owner for post-study follow-through so insights do not decay after the workshop high.
