Accessibility & inclusion · Case study

Inclusive by default

Moving accessibility from a late-stage audit to a shared standard in design, content, and front-end delivery.

Context

Releases were frequently “fixed” in a rushed remediation pass after accessibility came up in QA or from a customer report. That pattern taxed engineering, embarrassed the brand, and — most importantly — excluded users who rely on assistive technology or clear, predictable interactions. The organization wanted universal access to be a design principle, not a checkbox.

If inclusion is only inspected at the end, it is not inclusion — it is damage control.

My role

I partnered with design system owners and engineering leads to define what “done” meant for accessibility, contributed IA and UI patterns that met WCAG-oriented criteria, and helped socialize lightweight checks that teams could run before handoff.

Approach

Patterns before policing. We prioritized high-traffic components and templates — forms, navigation, modals, data tables — and documented keyboard flows, focus order, labels, and error handling so squads did not invent inconsistent accessibility every sprint.

Shift left with clarity. Design critiques included accessibility prompts (not lectures). Content designers weighed plain language and heading structure as part of the same review where we already discussed tone and conversion.

Measure what matters. We tracked reduction in severity-1 and severity-2 issues found in release testing, time spent in remediations, and participation in office hours — alongside qualitative feedback from internal advocates and users.

WCAG-aligned criteria tied to components
Earlier fixes issues caught in design & dev
Shared ownership design, content, engineering

Outcome

Critical accessibility escapes dropped release over release. Teams reported less thrash: fewer “surprise” violations and more predictable effort in estimation. New hires ramped faster because expectations lived in the system, not in tribal memory.

What I would tune next time

Even earlier partnership with legal and procurement on third-party tools — evaluating vendor accessibility up front saves painful retrofitting later.
